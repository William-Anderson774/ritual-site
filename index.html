TheChoreographyofthesmokebreak

Concept

This collection was born out of the concepts of trace and the evidence of the social choreography of the mundane. Every day, throughout whatever city, but especially here in Manhattan, we become part of a rushing, organized collective of people moving nonstop. En route to our meetings, classes, dinner parties, and scheduled appointments- our movement is largely dictated by our destination: where we are going, where we are coming from, where we need to be, and when our train is. Hidden within the bustle of the daily commute is another version of the day. One composed not in the rush of travel but of pauses, breaks, rests, and the moments of lingering. This book attempts to make those moments of the mundane visible through the physical remnants of one of those rest moments, the smoke break.

The collection began as street photography, documenting the unexpected and often funny compositions of the smoking habits of the city and the social dynamics that could be inferred from the evidence-three butts, a bag of chewed chicken wings, and a broken beer bottle. A cigarette smoked is a fleeting action, existing entirely in the moment of its performance, but the pack, butt, ash, match, lighter, or roach remains afterward, devoid of any value but a kind of record, a physical stamp of the process in which it was produced. Shown together, these remnants stop being trash and begin to suggest patterns of gathering and community interaction. One cigarette may mark one individual's break, but five gathered together in a corner just outside the entry of a building begin to imply a deeper conversation, a shared break, or a repeated ritual.

The smoke spot exists somewhere between public and private, charted and uncharted, scheduled and unscheduled. The photography shows these evidence sites from directly above. This choice was made to paint the street itself as the surface of documentation. The horizon vanishes, and the image flattens to become a diagram of the social movement, documenting how these objects come to occupy space in relation to each other.

The smoke break is particularly useful in exposing this hidden choreography because it interrupts the expected rhythm of the city. To smoke is to, momentarily, stop going somewhere. It produces a small pocket of time in which the demands of destination are suspended- five minutes outside a building, a conversation before returning to work, a cigarette after dinner, a moment alone before getting back on the train. These intervals may appear insignificant individually, but collectively, they form a large proportion of lived experience and represent the past actions of hundreds of thousands of individuals all around us.

Paired with academic texts, poetry, and journal articles about rest, lingering, social dynamics, and behavioral studies, this book is intended to function itself as an object of rest. The structure of the book, asymmetrical, with text and image separated, allows the audience to flip through at will. As you read, allow the images to draw attention, flip backward, let the narrative pull you in, and you decide when you take a break.
